What the telemetry could not separate, reading separates immediately:
the two lanes fail in opposite directions,
and which one is better depends on WHAT the archive got wrong rather than on
anything measurable about the slice.

Read 2026-08-20 at corpus `a41fc607ea5a70d8a7625cc67d5ed8c444f53379`.
`saurikissa` and `lintong` come from earlier readings recorded in
`doc/audit/the-critics-are-shown-the-wrong-paragraph.md`;

## The standard being applied

`doc/decision/translation-repair-output-goal.md`:
output is judged against the ORIGINAL,
not against how far it improved on the archive.
An archive rendering is EVIDENCE about what the original says and a starting
point worth keeping where it is right,
never the standard the result is measured by.
The same record decides that accurate detail a translator ADDED is kept rather
than stripped.

Both of those cut into the readings below,
in opposite directions.

## Where the translate lane won

SIX SLICES, and they also share a shape:
the archive INVENTED something, and the repair lane shipped the invention.

-   `Acheron` slice 1 is the clearest in the whole set.
    The Chinese says she had studied the technology field and names two videos.
    The archive invented a framing sentence about her being more than a gamer,
    invented a range from hardware to software,
    and invented that she often shared insights with the community.
    It also dropped the board's chipset and 鸿蒙 from its name.
    THE REPAIR LANE SHIPPED EVERY WORD OF THAT UNCHANGED.
    The translate lane removed all three inventions and restored both dropped
    identifiers.

-   `Weideriche_` slice 1.
    The archive invented "the difficulties of" and "with a smile" around a
    sentence that says only that she once faced life actively.
    The repair lane shipped that sentence verbatim.

-   `Acheron` slices 0 and 2.
    The archive turned 可爱 into "lovely", invented "life's challenges", lost
    one of the pair 乐观 and 积极, and narrowed "unwilling to let others be

    The repair lane kept the last three.

-   `gaoyanger` slice 1.
    The archive rendered 可爱 as "pretty" and 生活条件不好 as "poverty",

-   `Zha_Ke` slice 3.

-   `Weideriche_` slice 0, which carries the most serious single archive error

    girl where the Chinese says 人, person.
    BOTH LANES CORRECTED BOTH.

## The tally, and what it is worth

Thirteen slices where the lanes produced meaningfully different text:
four to the repair lane, six to the translate lane, three tied.
Six further slices were identical across both lanes, or carried no prose at all,
and are not counted: a spacer element, a photo component, two contributor lines,
a heading, and a one-line epigraph both lanes rendered identically.

THIS IS ONE READER'S JUDGEMENT, not a blind grading.
Earlier milestones calibrated agent grading against the owner's corrections
precisely because an agent's own calls drift.
Several here are close, and the owner may reverse individual ones.
What does NOT depend on the individual calls is the pattern, because the pattern
holds across entries and across both directions.

## The finding that matters

NEITHER LANE DOMINATES, AND THE REASON IS STRUCTURAL.

The repair lane's failure is INHERITANCE.
It edits the archive, so where the archive invented something and no critic
happened to flag it, the invention ships untouched.
Two entire fabricated sentences shipped this way in six entries.

The translate lane's failure is DISCARD.
It owes the archive nothing, so where the archive knew something the source
does not carry, that knowledge is lost:
a name in parentheses, and everything on a page whose content lives in pictures.

These are not two points on one scale.
A rule that prefers one lane, on any measurable property of a slice, is choosing
which of the two failures to accept everywhere.
That is why every automatic discriminator tried so far has been indefensible,
and it is a better explanation than the one recorded before this reading.

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY SEPARATE THEM is a question about the archive's text rather
than about either lane:
does this passage contain English the source does not support?
Where yes, the translate lane is right; where the archive carries accurate
detail beyond the source, or the source is not in the text at all, the repair
lane is.
Both are per-slice judgements about evidence, which is what the panel already
does, rather than properties a rule can read off.

## Costs neither lane fixes

TENSE IS INCONSISTENT WITHIN A PASSAGE, on a memorial page, in both lanes.
`lintong` alone runs present, past, past and present across four consecutive
clauses about the same person.
Chinese is tenseless, so the choice belongs to the renderer, and nothing in the
pipeline looks at it.

A STRAY CHARACTER in a source line divided the lanes with no rule to appeal to:
the repair lane preserved it on its own line where it reads as debris,
the translate lane dropped it silently.
Faithfulness to the original argues for keeping it and legibility argues for
dropping it, and nothing records that a choice was made.

THE ARCHIVE IS ALREADY INCONSISTENT ACROSS ENTRIES,
rendering the same section heading two different ways in this small sample.
A note recorded during this reading claimed the translate lane breaks a
cross-entry consistency; it does not, because there is none to break.
The translate lane diverged on that heading in two of five entries carrying it.
