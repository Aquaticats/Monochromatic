2. Selected text in the find and search boxes

Which ink should selected text use inside the find and search boxes?

The options differ in the dark scheme only; in the light scheme both draw selected text white. Measured on these frames, the darkest pixel of the selected text is 0 in a dark and the lightest is 255 in b dark (gray levels).

Find box

Ctrl+F, the find text Latin, then Ctrl+A.

Search box

Two Shift taps, the query fixture, then Ctrl+A.

What each option gains and costs

A: Toolkit colorsBuilt now

The toolkit's selection ink: black on the blue fill #0078D4 in the dark scheme (4.64:1), white in the light scheme (4.53:1).

Gains

Matches the toolkit's own text box.

Costs

In the dark scheme it differs from the white ink of the selected rows and of selected source text right beside the boxes.

B: Ink chosen from the fillRanked first

The rule of src/selection_ink.rs, used by the selected rows and selected source text: white on #0078D4 in both schemes (4.53:1).

Gains

One ink for every selection on the same fill.

Costs

Departs from the toolkit's text box; 0.11 lower contrast than black in the dark scheme.

Ranking: b > a: one ink for every selection on the same fill, and the contrast difference of 0.11 keeps both above the 4.5:1 minimum.

4. Clear cell while the x is hidden

While the clear control is hidden, should the box's trailing cell shrink to 12 px of padding or keep its 48 px?

The four states you asked for (focused and empty, typing, unfocused with a short text, unfocused and empty) are pixel-identical in both options, because the trailing cell is empty space whenever the x is hidden. The options differ only for a text longer than the box once focus leaves it, so those two states come first.

What each option gains and costs

A: Shrinks to 12 pxBuilt nowRanked first

While the x is hidden the cell is 12 px of padding, as in the toolkit's box.

Gains

Shows 36 px more of a long text without focus; matches the toolkit.

Costs

The visible end of a long text changes by 36 px when focus enters or leaves.

Costs

8. When the file-watch limit is reached

When the system's inotify watch limit is reached, should the IDE only log it, or also show a one-line message?

The message of b is triggered by a stand-in in the prototype; nothing exhausted the system's watches. With the limit reached, the IDE still notices changes through its timed rereads.

The window after start

What each option gains and costs

A: Log onlyBuilt nowRanked first

The log line Cannot watch <directory>: the inotify watch limit is reached; raise fs.inotify.max_user_watches; the window stays unchanged.

Gains

No extra interface, as the minimal-interface rule prefers.

Costs

You are not told why changes may take up to about a second to show.

B: Plus a one-line message

While the limit holds, a 32 px row along the bottom edge: a W letter tile in the warning color, a boundary above it, bold "File-watch limit reached:", and "changes may show up to 1 s late. Raise it with sudo sysctl fs.inotify.max_user_watches=1048576".

Gains

Names the limit and the remedy; appears only while the limit holds, which fits "an actual error may appear when needed" in the accepted scope.

Costs

Takes 32 px of height while shown and cannot be dismissed. Two wording caveats: the sysctl command raises the limit only until the next reboot, and "up to 1 s" holds only while fewer than about 50 folders are shown.

Ranking: a > b, the ranking most open to your veto: the effect of the limit is small and the minimal-interface rule leans to a; b is defensible because it is actionable and shown only while needed.
