Decision: cover subdirectory picker opened state

Tapping the folder trigger opens the subdirectory picker. The first round's K1 back navigation read as discouraging and the K3 sheet placed the choice far from its trigger, so this round crosses two trigger shapes with two containers, grounded in the Material 3 archive: chips appear in sets and are rounded rectangles, so the lone pill chip is gone; menus are the specified temporary surface and open from fields or buttons; sheets are for secondary content only. Every prototype reuses the settled picker presentation: D28 adaptive letter rail, D31 wrapped plain-text names at 48dp with primary plus 2dp indicator on the current folder, no segmentation, nothing truncated. Dark surfaces follow D41 and D42; light follows D45.

P1

Dropdown field plus floating menu panel

An outlined text field in exposed-dropdown form (56dp container, floating Folder label on the outline, 4dp corners, trailing dropdown icon, 12dp side and 16dp icon padding per the text-field specs) carries the folder name; the picker opens as an inset menu container with 4dp corners over the list, ending above the deck. The menu keeps the baseline 4dp corner but exceeds the 280dp menu width cap because the D31 name wall needs the full panel.

  • Pros: the field shape says choose one of many, matching the exposed dropdown lineage; the menu container is the specified temporary surface; the panel stays local to its trigger.
  • Cons: a second surface floats over the list; the list itself disappears behind the panel.
P2 · recommended

Dropdown field plus in-slot picker

The same outlined text-field trigger, but the picker replaces the list slot in place: rail and names occupy the list region while the deck stays visible; the caret flips to close.

  • Pros: one calm surface with no floating layer; the trigger never moves; the deck and its state stay visible; no back step and no scrim.
  • Cons: the track list is not visible while choosing.
P3

App-bar title plus floating menu panel

The folder name sits as the top row's title with a caret; the menu panel is identical to P1.

  • Pros: calmest top row; no extra container chrome.
  • Cons: plain title text is a weak trigger affordance; keeps the floating panel's cons.
P4

App-bar title plus in-slot picker

The title trigger with the in-slot container from P2.

  • Pros: calmest chrome overall; local; deck visible.
  • Cons: weakest trigger affordance of the four; list hidden while open.

Ranking: P2 > P4 > P1 > P3. P2 beats P4 because the outlined field states the selection semantics that a bare title only implies, at the cost of one bordered container. P4 beats P1 because keeping one calm surface in the list slot avoids a floating layer over content the person was just reading. P1 beats P3 because the floating menu at least pairs with a trigger that looks like a control.
